Chocolate comes from cacao beans. Cacao beans grow in pods. The pods grow on trees. 
The trees are in hot countries, near the equator.

The beans are fermented to develop flavor. Then they are dried and roasted. 
After roasting, the shells are removed. The inside is called a nib. Nibs are 
ground into a paste called chocolate liquor. It is not alcoholic; it is pure chocolate.

This liquor can be separated into cocoa solids and cocoa butter. Cocoa solids 
are brown and powdery. Cocoa butter is fatty and smooth. To make eating chocolate, 
you mix cocoa solids, cocoa butter, and sugar. Milk chocolate also has milk powder. 
White chocolate has cocoa butter, sugar, and milk, but no cocoa solids. Dark 
chocolate has more cocoa solids and is less sweet.

Chocolate is a popular flavor in cakes, cookies, ice cream, and candy bars. 
Hot chocolate is a drink. Chocolate is associated with holidays like Valentine's Day, 
Easter, Halloween, and Christmas. The history of chocolate is long. Ancient Mayans 
and Aztecs in Central America drank it bitter and spicy, considering it a gift from the gods. 
Spanish explorers brought it to Europe, where sugar was added. 

The first solid chocolate bar was made in the 1800s. Chocolate contains chemicals like 
theobromine and caffeine. Dark chocolate has antioxidants, but it also has fat and sugar, 
so it should be eaten in moderation. The chocolate industry has problems, including child 
labor on some cacao farms. Fair trade chocolate tries to address this issue.
