Sleep is when you close your eyes. Your body rests. Your mind rests. 
But your brain is still active. Sleep is not being awake. It is a different state. 
You need sleep to live. Without sleep, you get sick. Sleep has stages.

Light sleep is Stage 1. You drift off. Stage 2 is deeper. Stages 3 and 4 are deep sleep. 
Deep sleep is very restorative. Then there is REM sleep. REM stands for Rapid Eye 
Movement. Your eyes move fast under your eyelids. This is when you dream. 
Vivid dreams happen in REM. The sleep cycle repeats. Each cycle is about 90 minutes. 
 
You go through all stages. Then you start again. Adults need 7-9 hours per night. 
Children need more. Babies need a lot. Teenagers need a lot but often don't get it. 
Sleep is controlled by a body clock. This clock is in your brain. It responds to light. 
Light tells it to be awake. Darkness tells it to sleep. This is the circadian rhythm. 
 
Jet lag disrupts this rhythm. Shift work disrupts it. Good sleep hygiene helps. 
Go to bed at the same time. Wake up at the same time. Even on weekends. Make your 
bedroom dark. Make it quiet. Make it cool. Don't use screens before bed. The blue 
light from phones is bad. It tricks your brain into thinking it's daytime. 
 
Avoid caffeine in the afternoon. Avoid alcohol before bed. Alcohol might make 
you sleepy but ruins sleep quality. Exercise helps you sleep better. But not right 
before bed. A bedtime routine is good. Read a book. Take a warm bath. Relax. 
Your bed is for sleep and sex only. Not for work. Not for watching TV. This trains
your brain. Sleep is important for memory. Your brain files memories during sleep. 
  
It cleans out waste products. Sleep helps learning. If you study then sleep, you 
remember better. Lack of sleep is dangerous. Drowsy driving causes accidents. Sleep 
deprivation makes you moody. It hurts your immune system. You get sick easier. Long-term, 
it increases disease risk. Heart disease. Diabetes. Obesity. Sleep disorders exist.
