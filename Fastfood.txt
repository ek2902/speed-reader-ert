Fast food is a type of mass-produced food designed for commercial resale and with a strong 
priority placed on "speed of service" versus other relevant factors involved in culinary 
science. It is typically less nutritionally valuable compared to other foods and dishes. 
While any meal with low preparation time can be considered fast food, typically the term 
refers to food sold in a restaurant or store with preheated or precooked ingredients, 
and served to the customer in a packaged form for take-out/take-away.

Common fast-food items include hamburgers, french fries, fried chicken, pizza, hot dogs, 
and tacos. These items are often high in calories, saturated fat, sugar, and salt. The 
fast-food industry originated in the United States with White Castle in 1921. Today, 
corporations like McDonald's, KFC, Burger King, and Subway are multinational, with 
outlets across the globe.

Fast food is popular because it is convenient, cheap, and consistent. Drive-through 
service allows customers to order and receive food without leaving their cars. 
The business model relies on efficiency, standardization, and franchising. Food is often 
prepared in bulk and kept warm under heat lamps, or is quickly reheated, before serving. 
This system minimizes labor costs and maximizes speed.

The impact of fast food is widespread. It has been linked to the global rise in obesity 
and other health problems, such as heart disease and type 2 diabetes. In response, many 
fast-food chains now offer salads, grilled items, and fruit as healthier alternatives. 
There are also criticisms regarding environmental effects from excessive packaging, low 
wages for workers, and the homogenization of local food cultures.

and shapes modern eating habits. The industry constantly innovates with limited-time offers, 
breakfast menus, and delivery apps to stay competitive. Fast food is more than a meal;
